How do you talk in a group of people you just met?

Listen first, then ask one open question about what someone just said. Add a short story of your own, and you're in the conversation.

Start by listening

The first minute is for getting a feel for the group. Notice what they're laughing about.

You don't need a big opener. Joining in on the current topic is enough.

Ask one open question

Pick something that needs more than yes or no. "How did you get into that?" works almost anywhere.

Then actually listen to the answer. People remember who was curious about them.

Share a small story

Keep it to a few sentences and tie it to the topic. Then hand the conversation back with a question.

Short turns keep a group relaxed. Nobody wants a speech at a first meeting.

Practice on voice alone

Without photos, there's less to second-guess, and you can focus on what people say. That's the idea behind Table for 4 on TopNote: a 10-minute voice chat with 3 other members on one topic.

You can leave anytime. Afterward, if two people quietly pick each other, they get a 3-minute 1:1 call.

FAQ

What if I'm shy in groups?
Start by asking one person one easy question. Smaller steps feel much less scary than speaking to the whole circle.
How do I jump in without interrupting?
Wait for a natural pause, then build on what someone just said. A quick "That reminds me of..." is a friendly way in.
Is there a way to practice group conversation?
Yes. A Table for 4 on TopNote is a 10-minute voice chat with 3 other members on one topic, and you can leave anytime.
