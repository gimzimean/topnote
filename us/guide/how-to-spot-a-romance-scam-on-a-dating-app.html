How do you spot a romance scam on a dating app?

Be wary of anyone who rushes into love talk, pushes you off the app, or asks for money. Any one of these is a reason to slow down, and money is the clearest one.

Moving too fast

Pet names and "I love you" within days can be a way to build trust quickly. Real feelings usually take a little time.

Pushing you off the app

Be careful if they want to switch to another messaging app right away. The app's report and block tools stop working once you leave.

Dodging a live call

Watch for endless excuses to skip a voice or video call. Hearing someone live, even for a few minutes, is one of the easiest checks. See our guides on avoiding catfishing and a call before a first date.

Any ask for money

Medical bills, travel, a deposit, an investment tip: don't send money to someone you haven't met in person. Gift cards, crypto and wire transfers are especially hard to get back.

Where TopNote fits

On TopNote, your first contact is a 3-minute voice call, and every member has gone through a face check and team review. That doesn't guarantee anyone's intentions, so keep the same common sense.

FAQ

Is one red flag enough to call it a scam?
Not always, but take it seriously. Several together, especially anything about money, are a strong reason to step back.
Should I ever send money to someone I haven't met?
No. Gift cards, crypto and wire transfers are the usual asks, so a firm no is the safest answer.
What should I do if I think I'm talking to a scammer?
Stop replying, then report and block them in the app. You can also report it to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
